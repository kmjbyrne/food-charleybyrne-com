This recipe is really two sauces. Everything up to the pressure cook is the
cooked backbone, which takes heat and time. Everything in the finish is fresh
and dies if you cook it. Keep the two apart, and you fix a curry that tastes
close but flat.

## Ingredients

### Meat

- 800g boneless lamb shoulder, cut in chunks

### Cooked Base

- 3 tbsp mustard oil
- 1 tsp jimbu (optional)
- 1 tsp cumin seeds
- 3 medium onions, roughly chopped
- 6 garlic cloves
- 30g ginger, roughly chopped
- 150ml water, for blending

### Dry Spices

- 1/2 tsp turmeric
- 1 tsp ground cumin
- 1 tsp ground coriander
- 1 1/4 tsp salt

### Raw Green Paste

- 40g fresh coriander, leaves and stems
- 3 green chillies
- 2 garlic cloves
- 10g ginger

### Finish

- 50g cashew nuts, soaked
- 60ml double cream
- 1/2 tsp timur, toasted and ground
- 1 lemon, juiced

## Method

### Prep the Pastes

1. Cover the cashews with just-boiled water and leave them to soften while you
   do everything else. This is what gives the sauce its body later, not the
   cream.
2. Blend the fresh coriander, green chillies, 2 garlic cloves and 10g ginger
   with a splash of water into a loose, bright paste. Set aside. This does not
   go near the pressure cooker.

### Build the Base

1. Heat the mustard oil in the open cooker until it shimmers and the raw
   pungency burns off. It will smoke lightly. Take it off the heat for 2 minutes
   so you do not scorch the aromatics.
2. Back on medium, add the jimbu and cumin seeds and let them sizzle 15-20
   seconds until fragrant.
3. Add the onions, 6 garlic cloves and 30g ginger. Fry, stirring, until deep
   golden, past the point that feels comfortable. With no tomato, this browning
   is where the body and colour come from. Do not rush it.
4. Tip the browned mixture into a blender with the 150ml water and blitz smooth.
   Return it to the cooker.
5. Stir in the turmeric, ground cumin, ground coriander and salt, then add the
   lamb. Keep it looser than you want the final sauce, nothing evaporates under
   pressure. Lock the lid, high pressure 25 minutes, then natural release for
   about 10 minutes.

### Finish Off the Heat

1. Open up and simmer uncovered until the sauce thickens and turns glossy. This
   reduction is where it stops looking like stew.
2. Blend the soaked cashews smooth with a splash of their water and stir in,
   followed by the cream. Turn the heat low, stir through the reserved green
   paste and the timur, and warm 1-2 minutes only. Do not boil it green.
3. Off the heat, stir in the lemon juice. Taste for salt and lemon, and serve.

## Notes

- Soak the cashews whole, but never add them whole. Blend them smooth first.
  Cream adds richness. Cashew paste adds structure, which cream cannot do on its
  own. Use a stick blender in a narrow jug, not a big jug blender.
- Split the garlic and ginger on purpose. The cooked amounts build depth. The
  raw ones in the green paste keep the fresh bite. Keep them in separate piles
  while you prep.
- Timur and lemon are late high notes. Both die if you cook them. Timur is the
  restaurant note, so order it online if you cannot buy it locally.
- Most people underdo the onion browning at step 5. If it looks pale going into
  the blender, the curry tastes thin. Nothing you add later fixes that.
- No pressure cooker? Slow cook the lamb instead. At step 7, cover and simmer
  gently for 2 to 2.5 hours. A low oven at 150°C takes about 3 hours. The lamb
  is ready when it gives with no resistance. Do not rush it at a hard boil,
  because lamb shoulder goes tough before it goes tender. Check the liquid every
  40 minutes and top it up.
- Any meat works here, but only slow cuts need the pressure cooker. Chicken
  thigh takes 5-6 minutes under pressure at most. It is just as good simmered
  uncovered for 15-20 minutes at step 7. Add prawns at step 9 with the cream and
  let the residual heat cook them. Lamb on the bone takes the full 25 minutes.
