Espagnole takes the longest of the five. You need a brown roux, a properly
coloured mirepoix and a few hours of reduction. Reduce it again with more stock
and it becomes demi-glace.

## Ingredients

- 60g butter
- 60g plain flour
- 1 small onion, diced
- 1 carrot, diced
- 1 stick celery, diced
- 2 tbsp tomato purée
- 1.5 litres brown stock, veal or beef, warm
- 1 bay leaf, few sprigs thyme, few parsley stalks

## Method

1. Melt the butter and stir in the flour. Cook over medium-low heat 15 to 20
   minutes to a brown roux, the colour of peanut butter. This cannot be rushed
   and must not catch.
2. In a separate pan, colour the onion, carrot and celery hard. Pale mirepoix
   gives a pale sauce.
3. Stir the tomato purée into the vegetables and cook 2 minutes until it darkens
   and loses its raw edge.
4. Whisk the warm stock into the roux a ladle at a time until smooth.
5. Add the vegetables and the herbs. Simmer very gently 2 to 3 hours, skimming
   often. It should reduce by about a third.
6. Strain through a fine sieve, pressing the solids lightly.

## Notes

- Brown roux thickens less than blond for the same weight, because the starch
  breaks down as it colours. Use a bit more of it, or reduce the sauce longer.
- Skim often. Fat and scum left in the pan will turn the sauce muddy.

## Derivatives

Demi-glace is espagnole reduced again with more brown stock. Bordelaise adds red
wine, shallot and marrow. Chasseur adds mushroom, shallot and white wine. Robert
adds onion and mustard.
