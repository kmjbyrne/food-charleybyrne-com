Velouté is stock plus roux, nothing more. The character comes entirely from the
stock, so a weak one gives you thickened water. It is the base for suprême,
allemande and a great many pan sauces.

## Ingredients

- 60g butter
- 60g plain flour
- 1 litre white stock, chicken, veal or fish, warm
- Salt and white pepper

## Method

1. Melt the butter in a heavy pan over medium-low heat.
2. Stir in the flour and cook 3 to 4 minutes to a blond roux. It should smell
   biscuity and turn the colour of pale straw. Do not let it brown.
3. Add the warm stock a ladle at a time, whisking each addition smooth before
   the next. Adding it cold or all at once is what causes lumps.
4. Simmer gently 25 to 30 minutes, skimming what rises. It should coat a spoon.
5. Strain through a fine sieve. Season with salt and white pepper.

## Notes

- White pepper, not black. Black specks in a pale sauce look like a mistake.
- The roux and the stock should never both be hot. One warm, one cool is the
  reliable rule.

## Derivatives

Supreme adds cream to a chicken veloute. Allemande is finished with egg yolk and
lemon. Bercy adds shallots and white wine. Normande, from fish veloute, adds
cream and mushroom liquor.
