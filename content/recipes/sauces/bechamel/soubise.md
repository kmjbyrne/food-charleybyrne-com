Onion cooked down until very soft, then blended smooth. It comes out sweet, pale
and silky. Good with lamb, eggs and white fish.

There are two ways to make it. Escoffier builds it on bechamel, which is why it
sits among the bechamel daughters. The modern shortcut skips the roux and
reduces cream with the onions instead. The cream version is quicker and tastes
cleaner of onion; the bechamel version holds better on a plate and reheats
without splitting.

## Ingredients

### Classical, on bechamel

:sub-recipe{path="/recipes/sauces/bechamel" section="Ingredients" variant="Classic" label="Bechamel base"}

- 2 large onions, finely sliced
- 30g butter
- 50ml cream, optional
- Salt and white pepper

### Keto, on keto bechamel

:sub-recipe{path="/recipes/sauces/bechamel" section="Ingredients" variant="Keto" label="Keto bechamel base"}

- 2 large onions, finely sliced
- 30g butter
- Salt and white pepper

### Cream, no roux

- 2 medium onions, thinly sliced
- 30g unsalted butter
- 350ml double cream
- 1 tsp curry powder or vadouvan, optional
- Salt and ground white pepper

## Method

### Classical

1. Melt the butter over low heat. Add the onions with a pinch of salt.
2. Cover and sweat 25 to 30 minutes, stirring now and then. Keep them pale, so
   drop the heat if they start to colour.
3. Stir the onions into the warm bechamel and simmer 10 minutes.
4. Blend smooth, then pass through a fine sieve.
5. Stir in the cream if using. Season.

### Keto

1. Melt the butter over low heat. Add the onions with a pinch of salt.
2. Cover and sweat 25 to 30 minutes, keeping them pale.
3. Stir the onions into the warm keto bechamel and simmer 5 minutes. It is
   already thick, so it needs less time than the classical version.
4. Blend smooth and pass through a fine sieve. Season.

### Cream

1. Melt the butter over low heat and add the onions with a good pinch of salt.
2. Cover and sweat 25 to 30 minutes until completely soft and still pale.
3. Stir in the curry powder if using and cook 1 minute.
4. Add the cream and simmer gently 10 to 15 minutes until it thickens and coats
   a spoon.
5. Blend smooth and pass through a sieve. Season with salt and white pepper.

## Notes

- Keep the onion pale in both versions. Browned onion makes it taste like French
  onion soup and turns the sauce beige.
- The cream version depends on reduction for its body, so do not cut the
  simmering short. It thickens noticeably as it cools.
- The keto version inherits the keto bechamel, which is already thick from the
  cream cheese. Simmer it less or it turns claggy.
- Vadouvan is the interesting option in the cream version. It is a French
  adaptation of a masala, so it sits naturally in an onion sauce.
