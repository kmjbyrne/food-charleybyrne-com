## Equipment

- 8 inch springform pan
- Roasting tin (for oven steam)
- Blender or spice grinder (for the sweetener blend)
- Stand or hand mixer

## Ingredients

Choose keto or standard at the top of the page. The filling and method are the
same either way; only the crust and the sweetener change.

### Keto crust: almond and pecan

- 95g almond flour
- 40g pecans, finely crushed
- 55g unsalted butter, melted
- 1 1/2 tbsp powdered erythritol
- Tiny pinch of stevia
- Pinch of salt
- Lemon zest, or 1 tbsp lemon juice

### Keto crust: coconut and pecan

- 45g coconut flour
- 40g pecans, finely crushed
- 65g unsalted butter, melted
- 1 1/2 tbsp powdered erythritol
- Tiny pinch of stevia
- Pinch of salt
- Lemon zest, or 1 tbsp lemon juice

### Keto crust: plain almond

- 135g almond flour
- 50g unsalted butter, melted
- 1 1/2 tbsp powdered erythritol
- Tiny pinch of stevia
- Pinch of salt
- Lemon zest, or 1 tbsp lemon juice

### Standard crust: digestive biscuit

- 200g digestive biscuits, crushed to crumbs
- 100g unsalted butter, melted
- Pinch of salt
- Lemon zest, or 1 tbsp lemon juice

### Filling

- ~900g full-fat cream cheese, at room temperature (3 packs of 275g is close
  enough, this is not a precise measure)
- 1 pack (250g) mascarpone
- 150g sour cream, or 150ml double cream for a looser, silkier set
- 3 large eggs (~165g)
- 1 1/2 tbsp (~11g) coconut flour
- 1 tbsp fresh lemon juice
- 1 vanilla bean, scraped, or 1 1/2 tsp vanilla paste
- Sweetener, see below

### Keto sweetener

- 245g erythritol
- 0.75g (~1/4 tsp) pure stevia Reb 98% B

### Standard sweetener

- 200g caster sugar

## Method

1. For keto, blitz the erythritol and stevia together in a blender until fine
   and chalky. Gritty sweetener does not dissolve in the filling. Caster sugar
   needs no blitzing.
2. Heat the fan oven to 155°C.
3. Mix your chosen crust ingredients until the texture of wet sand. If using the
   coconut option, rest it 2 minutes so the flour absorbs the butter.
4. Press firmly into the base of the springform pan with the flat bottom of a
   glass.
5. Bake 10-12 minutes for either almond crust, 8-10 minutes for coconut or
   digestive, until lightly golden. Cool completely on the counter.
6. Put an empty roasting tin on the bottom oven rack and set another rack on the
   middle shelf above it. Pour 2-3cm of boiling water into the tin and shut the
   door straight away to trap the steam.
7. Beat the cream cheese and the powdered sweetener blend on low for 2 minutes
   until smooth.
8. Mix in the coconut flour, mascarpone, sour cream and vanilla, just until
   combined.
9. Add the eggs one at a time on low speed, stopping as each disappears. Stir in
   the lemon juice for 5-10 seconds at the very end. Do not over-mix or you will
   trap air.
10. Tap the bowl on the counter 3-4 times to pop air bubbles, then pour over the
    cooled crust.
11. Set the pan on the middle rack, directly above the water tray. Bake at 155°C
    fan for 40 minutes with the door closed.
12. Drop to 145°C fan and bake another 25-35 minutes. Look for matte, set edges
    and a 5-7cm wobble in the centre. If it is fully set or browning, stop
    there.
13. Turn the oven off, prop the door open 2-3cm with a wooden spoon and leave
    the cheesecake inside for 1 hour.
14. Move to the counter. Run a thin flexible knife around the inside rim so the
    cake does not stick as it contracts. Leave until the pan is cool to the
    touch.
15. Cover and refrigerate 8-12 hours, overnight ideally, before slicing.

## Notes

- The plain almond crust uses more flour and less butter than the pecan
  versions, because the pecans were carrying both fat and bulk. It bakes
  slightly firmer and tastes cleaner, which suits a plain vanilla filling.
- The pan goes into the oven unwrapped. The steam tray below does the job of a
  water bath with no risk of a leaking foil seal.
- The coconut flour in the filling is a thickener, not a flavour. It soaks up
  the moisture that would otherwise leave a keto cheesecake loose.
